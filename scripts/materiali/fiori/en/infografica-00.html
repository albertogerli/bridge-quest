Bridge: A Trick-Taking Game

Two partnerships, 52 cards, thirteen tricks to win: the side that bids highest in the auction chooses how the hand is played.

1

The deck and the partnerships

one hand: 13 cards

Bridge is a partnership game: the player across the table is your partner, so it's North-South against East-West. The deck has 52 cards in four suits of 13, from the ace down to the two.

♠ Spades♥ Hearts♦ Diamonds♣ Clubs
Honors: A K Q J 10. Spot cards: 9 down to 2.

No cards in a suit: a void. One card: a singleton. Two: a doubleton.

2

The trick

The first card played is the opening lead. The others, in clockwise order, must follow suit: play a card of the same suit.

The highest card of the suit led wins the trick.

West leads the K♠, North plays the 3, East the 7: South wins with the ace. A player out of the suit discards another card, and a discard never wins the trick.

3

Dummy and declarer

After the opening lead, dummy lays its cards face up on the table, sorted by suit, and takes no further part: its partner, declarer, chooses every card.

Declarer sees 26 cards: their own 13 and dummy's 13. That makes it possible to plan the play.
The defenders see only their own cards and dummy's, never their partner's.
4

Trumps or notrump

Trumps: one suit gets special power. A player out of the suit led can ruff with a trump and win the trick.
Ruffing: only when you can't follow suit, and never compulsory. If two players ruff, the higher trump wins.
Notrump (NT): no suit can ruff. A player out of a suit can only discard.
5

The auction and the contract

2♥

Through an auction a side commits to taking a number of tricks: the highest bid chooses how the hand is played. The number counts tricks beyond the first six: "2♥" means at least 8 tricks, with hearts as trumps.

From the top:NT›♠›♥›♦›♣after 1♥ you can bid 1♠; 1♦ and 1♣ need the 2 level
When a bid is followed by three passes, that bid becomes the contract.
