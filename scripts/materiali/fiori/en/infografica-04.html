The No Trump Play Plan

Your winners are rarely enough: the plan tells you how many tricks are missing, where to find them and how to cash them.

1

Four questions after the opening lead

1How many sure tricks do I have?
2How many more does the contract need?
3Which suits can provide them?
4How do I get them?
2

Entries and communication

Entry: the card that wins the trick in the right hand at the right time.
Link: the card that, thanks to the entry, carries the lead from one hand to the other.
NORTH
SOUTH
3NT · West leads the 2♥

Winners: 2 spades, 2 hearts, 1 club. Diamonds give 4 more after losing to the ace.

  1. Win with the king of hearts: the ace stays with the diamonds.
  2. Lead South's diamond honors first, from the short side.
Protect the entries next to the long suit you want to establish.
3

Count the opponents' tricks too

NORTH
SOUTH
3NT · West leads the K♥

5 winners, 4 more needed. Clubs would provide them, but only by losing to the ace: after the lead the defenders have at least 4 hearts to cash, and with the A♣ that makes 5. Take the diamond finesse instead: it doesn't give up the lead.

4

Win in the right hand

NORTH
SOUTH
3NT · West leads the 2♦

Before winning the lead, decide your next move. Clubs must be led from North toward South, to catch the king with East: so win with dummy's ace of diamonds and lead the queen of clubs.

5

The duck

South has few entries. If declarer plays A, K and another club, the two good clubs have no entry. Better to duck at once: the 2 to the 3. Then the A, a club to the K and all the rest.

Give up early a trick you'd lose anyway, to keep communication in the suit.

Unblocking a suit

NORTH
SOUTH
West leads the K♠

13 winners, but the singleton ace blocks the diamonds. There is an entry: dummy's 10 of hearts. South cashes the A♦, plays the 2♥ to the 10♥ and runs the K Q J 10 of diamonds.

Blocked suit? Use the other suits to get around the block.
