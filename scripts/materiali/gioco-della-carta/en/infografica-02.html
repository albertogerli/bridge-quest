Card play

The Defenders' Perspective

The defense has a plan too: it starts with the opening lead and goes on by reading the cards as they fall.

1

The goal

Defeat the contract

If North-South need 9 tricks, East-West need only 5.

The opening lead is an edge

The player on declarer's left makes it: it is the first move of the defensive plan.

No hurry

Like declarer, the defense does not cash its winners at once: they are needed to establish tricks.

2

Which suit to lead

Your longest suit. With two of equal length, the one with better honors.
Four hearts and four diamonds:
hearts, better honors → Q♥
The long suit is spades
→ 2♠
Clubs, no doubt → K♣.
Cashing the aces gives tricks away.
3

Which card to lead

Honors: A K Q J 10Spot cards: 9 down to 2

1 · Top of a sequence

Three high cards in a row, or nearly: KQJ, QJ10, KQ10, AQJ. Lead the highest of the touching cards.

2 · Low from an honor

At least the jack but no sequence: lead your lowest card.

3 · Second highest

No sequence and nothing above the 10: lead the second card from the top.

4

Third hand

Try to win. West leads the 4: East plays the queen. With a low card, South wins the 10 and takes two tricks.
Don't block. West leads the king: East overtakes with the ace and returns the suit. West cashes five tricks.
Return partner's suit whenever you can, unless dummy and your own cards say to switch.
5

Third hand: lowest

With equal cards, third hand plays the lowest. The player who leads plays the highest.

6

The leader's inference

The card third hand plays to win the trick denies the card just below it.

You lead the 3, East plays the jack, South wins with the queen.

what you see
what you infer

With J10 East would have played the 10, with the ace East would have played it: South has the 10 and the ace. Don't lead the suit again; wait for partner to lead it.
