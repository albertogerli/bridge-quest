The Play Plan with Trumps

The opponents can ruff too. The plan decides when to draw trumps, when to wait, what comes first.

1

On defense: leading against a trump contract

Quick tricks

A long suit helps little: declarer will ruff it. Two touching honors are enough to lead from a sequence.
against 4♥: the K♠, better than the 2♦.

Looking for a ruff

Lead a short suit to get a ruff. From two cards, always the higher: from 74 the 7, from 102 the 10, from A9 the ace.
against 4♥: the 2♠.

Never underlead an ace

Against 4♥ the J♣ lets South's singleton king win, and the ace never scores.
2

Declarer: draw trumps?

With long side-suit winners or establishable cards, draw trumps: otherwise they'll get ruffed.

Spades are trumps, hearts are the side suit: the more cards your side holds, the likelier an honor gets ruffed.

3

Only short winners? Ruff

NORTH
SOUTH
2♦ · West leads the Q♥

You need 8 tricks; the side suits give 3. Drawing trumps, diamonds take 4, never 5. So: A♥, heart ruffed with the 4; spade to the ace, heart ruffed with the 5; spade to the king, heart ruffed with the 8. Hearts 4-4: 8 tricks.

4

Wait to draw trumps

NORTH
SOUTH
4♠ · West leads the 2♣

9 tricks, and drawing trumps leaves 9. At once A♥ and a heart; later a heart ruffed in dummy: 10 tricks. Even one round of trumps would be fatal: on lead with a heart, a defender plays a trump and the ruff is gone.

Wait to draw trumps if you need ruffs in the short hand.

6

Urgent things first

4♠, West leads the K♥. Win and draw trumps, and the defense takes two hearts, the A♠ and the A♣: down one. Instead, after the A♥, South cashes K♦, Q♦, A♦ discarding a heart: the third heart can now be ruffed.

When your plan gives up the lead, always ask what the opponents will do next.
